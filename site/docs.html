Install

Download the .dmg, drag Pixie to Applications, then right-click → Open the first time. (Windows .exe and Linux .AppImage previews are on the Download page; on Windows turn on Settings → Privacy & security → Speech → Online speech recognition so Pixie can hear you. What works where.) The :) appears in the menu bar and the welcome window starts: your name, permissions, a model key, a mic test, and the shortcuts. The natural voice downloads in the background while you go.

Models and keys

The short ones (quit, stop working, never mind, new chat, switch to Eon) work instantly without asking the model. API keys, permissions and Google sign-in can't be changed by voice: Pixie opens the page for you.

The buddy

The round :) rides just off your pointer. It points at real controls by name, draws circles, underlines and selections, and clicks or types when you ask. Settings → General: Always on, Idle (only while Pixie is busy) or Off; and whether it may click for you.

Memory and today's chat

Today's chat carries on all day: after a quiet hour or a restart, Pixie still knows what you were talking about (and how long it's been). It ends when you press New chat in the notch, say “new chat”, or the day changes: at midnight, or the hour you pick in Settings → Memory. The last 12 turns go to the model in full and older ones as a short summary, so long days stay quick. Every chat is kept in the Jobs window.

Long-term memory is a set of short notes about you: who you are, how you like answers, your people, projects and setup. Say “remember that…” or “forget…”. On Automatic, Pixie also reads a chat once it's been quiet for a few minutes and keeps anything lasting; Only when I ask keeps just what you tell it; Off stops reading and writing notes. The most useful notes go with every question and Pixie searches the rest when they matter. In Settings → Memory you can add, edit (click a note), search and forget notes, or forget everything. Notes live in memory.json on your computer; passwords, keys and card numbers are refused.

Agents

Background agents work on their own (research, documents, files, code) and keep a live note. Live agents do the task on your screen and explain each step. Both show in the notch's Agents tab; past jobs and chats are in the Jobs window. Agents never overwrite your files and can't touch .ssh, .aws, keychains or .env files.

Apps and accounts

Settings → Apps switches on Calendar, Reminders, Contacts and Apple Mail; each asks macOS once. Then just ask: “what's on tomorrow?”, “remind me to call Sam at 5”, “draft a reply to Alex”. Drafts open for you to send; Pixie never sends mail.

Google: create an OAuth client of type Desktop app in Google Cloud, enable the Gmail and Calendar APIs, paste the client ID and secret, and sign in. Pixie reads Gmail, saves drafts and reads or adds events; tokens stay on your Mac.

A green dot means connected, with the number of tools; What it can do lists them. If one can't connect, the card says why in plain words, with Retry and the server's own message under Details. Add your own server takes any MCP server's command (and environment, one KEY=value per line) without editing mcp.json by hand. Connectors run through Node.js; tokens stay on your computer. Pixie asks before anything that posts, sends or deletes.

Updates

Pixie updates itself from GitHub. It checks when it starts and every six hours, downloads a new version in the background, checks its signature, and switches over when you aren't using it. Settings → About shows where it is and has Check now and Restart now; the menu-bar menu has Check for Updates. Turn off Update automatically to decide yourself.

Canvas

When a picture explains it better, Pixie draws on a real Excalidraw canvas in its own window: boxes, arrows, text, or a Mermaid flowchart laid out for you. Everything stays editable. Name it at the top and save it as .excalidraw, PNG or SVG (to Downloads, never overwriting; Show reveals the file).

How often: Settings → General → Drawing: Only when I ask, When it helps (the default: flows, steps, how things connect) or Often. Or say “draw more” or “only draw when I ask”.

The window comes up in front of you, also over a full-screen app, and goes away when you click outside it. Bring it back with Show Canvas in the menu-bar menu, or ask: “open the canvas”, “open my git canvas”, “list my canvases”. Pixie can read your edits back (“add a database box under the server”), clear a canvas, or delete one or all of them; Delete in the canvas does it too (click twice), and deleted canvases go to the Trash.

Pick one in Settings → General → Personality, or say “switch to Inos”, “be Eon”, “turn on spicy”. The face changes in the notch, on the buddy and in Settings. A personality changes only how replies sound: same voice, same tools, same care before sending or deleting. Documents and reports that agents write keep the plain style.

Voice and sounds

The natural voice runs offline (about 370 MB, downloaded during setup); the system voice works meanwhile. To stay light it loads when you start talking (about a second, while you speak) and unloads after five quiet minutes, so Pixie uses about 215 MB when idle. Settings → Sounds lets you change, quieten or replace every sound with your own file.

Privacy

Speech recognition, screen OCR and the natural voice run on your Mac. Screenshots and your words go only to the model provider you chose, with your key. Chats, memory notes, canvases and usage are kept locally; you can delete any of them. There is no Pixie server.

Troubleshooting

"Can't be opened": right-click → Open, or System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. Permission not sticking: Settings → Permissions → Reset, allow, relaunch. No answer: check the key in Settings → Models. Pixie remembers something wrong: say “forget that…”, or delete the note in Settings → Memory. The canvas vanished: it hides when you click outside; Show Canvas in the menu-bar menu brings it back. Pixie draws too much: Settings → General → Drawing → Only when I ask. Still stuck? Open an issue.
