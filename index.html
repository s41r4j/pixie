A helper that lives in your notch. And just helps.

Hold ⌃ ⌥ and talk. openclicky sees what's on your screen, points at the right button, sketches an explanation, or quietly takes on the bigger job while you keep working.

Features

Everything happens where you already are.

No chat window to switch to. It answers out loud, shows you on the screen, and gets out of the way.

Talk, don't type

Hold control and option, ask, let go. You hear the answer in a natural voice a second later.

Sees and points

“Where's the export button?” A pointer flies out of the notch and lands right on it.

Explains on a board

Ask why something works and it sketches the idea on a clean whiteboard while it talks you through it.

Jobs in the background

“Make me a one-page PDF about tea.” A card shows the plan; a live note fills in; the file lands where you asked.

Dictation anywhere

Hold fn and control and speak. Clean, punctuated text is typed into whatever field you're in.

Private by default

Speech recognition and the voice run on your Mac. Screens and files only leave it when a question needs a model.

Hover the notch

Talk, type, your running jobs and the last answer, one glance away.

A board for explanations

Diagrams get their own paper, so nothing covers what it's drawing.

Notes, not chat

Every job writes a page: the answer first, then the details, with buttons for what's next.

Cards on the edge

See progress at a glance. When a job needs you, it asks once, with tap answers.

How it works

Running in two minutes.

Download

Open the DMG and drag openclicky to Applications. It lives in your menu bar, not your Dock.

Allow four switches

Microphone, speech, screen and accessibility. The welcome screen walks you through each one.

Add your key

Paste a Claude or DeepSeek API key in Settings. You pay your provider directly; openclicky costs nothing.

git clone https://github.com/openclicky/openclicky && cd openclicky
npm install && npm run app:build && npm run app:install
Free

Free. Open source. Yours.

No subscription, no account, no usage meter. The code is MIT licensed, so you can read it, change it and ship your own.
